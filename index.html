A Poem

A Pact

by Ezra Pound

I make a pact with you, Walt Whitman—
I have detested you long enough.
I come to you as a grown child
Who has had a pig-headed father;
I am old enough now to make friends.
It was you that broke the new wood,
Now is a time for carving.
We have one sap and one root—
Let there be commerce between us.

First published in Poetry, April 1913, under Harriet Monroe's editorship. Public domain.

Principles

The open door, and a few don'ts.

Monroe promised that the door would never be shut against the great poet still to come, and that the magazine would print the best verse being written, regardless of where, by whom, or under what theory of art. Pound, for his part, gave three rules in 1913 that still hold:

  1. Direct treatment of the “thing,” whether subjective or objective.
  2. To use absolutely no word that does not contribute to the presentation.
  3. As regarding rhythm: to compose in the sequence of the musical phrase, not in sequence of a metronome.
